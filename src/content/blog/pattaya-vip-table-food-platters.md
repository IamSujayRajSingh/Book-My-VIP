Book My VIP’s Pattaya package summaries list four sharing platters: fruit, vegetarian, non-vegetarian and chakhna. The summaries name the platters, but leave the ingredients, portion sizes and serving time to be confirmed. If the group wants dinner at the club, ask for those details before deciding where to eat.

## Will the platters feed the whole group?

The same four platter descriptions appear across the [three package summaries](/vip-packages/). A larger table price tells you little about the size of a serving. Ask how many people each platter is intended to serve and whether all four come out together. If two friends are arriving later, find out whether one platter can be served after they arrive.

For a group planning a full meal, compare the venue’s answer with what your guests want. One person may be happy to share snacks while another expects a main course. A dinner reservation beforehand can settle that question without changing the table package.

## What is on the chakhna platter?

The word is the name used on the package list. Book My VIP’s summary does not specify a recipe. Ask for the actual items for your venue and date. A photo or menu description from the venue can be more useful to the group than the platter name alone.

## How do you ask about dietary needs?

Give the venue the specific ingredient or preparation question. For example, a guest who avoids eggs can ask which items contain egg and whether alternatives are available. A vegetarian label alone cannot establish whether an item meets a guest’s requirements. For an allergy, request a direct answer from the kitchen about ingredients and preparation before deciding whether to eat it.

Use the additional requests field in the [Pattaya enquiry form](/contact/#inquiry) to put the question to the team. Ask them to include any agreed substitution and its price in the final summary.
