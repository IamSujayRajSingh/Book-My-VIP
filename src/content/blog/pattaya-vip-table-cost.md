## What does the price per guest tell you?

An even split gives friends a useful number to discuss before anyone commits. Divide the agreed table total by the number of people who will actually contribute. If five guests are paying for a sixth guest’s birthday, use five as your divisor. If someone is still deciding, work out both totals before collecting money.

There are two different questions to put to the concierge. First, can the table seat everyone on your date? Second, what will the whole group owe for that arrangement? Dividing a package price cannot answer the first question. This matters especially for VIP Gold, where the listed summary leaves capacity open.

## Which costs belong in the table quote?

Ask for one written figure in Thai baht and enough detail to reconcile it. Check the table package, any applicable cover, and charges for requests such as a cake. If a deposit is requested, ask whether it counts toward that total, how much remains payable at the venue, and what happens if the headcount changes. Keep transport and dinner in a separate part of your budget.

## How should a group choose between similar quotes?

Start with the things you will actually use. A group celebrating over dinner may care more about seating than sharing platters. A birthday group might value a preferred song, provided the venue can accommodate the request. Compare the [published package summaries](/vip-packages/) side by side, then ask for the final offer for your date before splitting the bill.
